At the start of the semester, our instructor made one thing clear: by the end of it, we would be building a complete game from scratch. He walked us briefly through everything we would need to get there, including the core concepts, project planning and management, the technical skills, and the resources available to us. It was a lot to take in on day one, but it set the expectation early that this was a semester-long build rather than a series of isolated assignments.

Midway through, our first draft was coming due and the project had barely moved. With the deadline closing in, I ended up carrying roughly ninety percent of that draft myself. It was a difficult stretch, and it taught me something about what happens when a team's workload is not distributed and no one has claimed ownership of the schedule.

As the semester wrapped up, two of my five teammates stepped up and helped carry the project across the finish line. The whole thing was a roller coaster, and at the time it was genuinely unpleasant. Looking back, though, it was one of the more valuable experiences I had. It taught me how a development team actually functions under pressure, how uneven contribution gets resolved, and how much of software engineering has nothing to do with writing code. That is a lesson I would not have gotten from a solo assignment.
