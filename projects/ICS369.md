Throughout the semester, the coursework came in weekly assignments, with a few stretched across two weeks. Early on, the focus was on learning Maya by hand: building assets, setting keyframes, adjusting them in the timeline, and rendering the result out as a video. Doing it manually meant clicking through the interface for every change, which was slow, but it forced me to understand what each control actually did before I ever tried to automate it.

Later in the semester, the assignments gradually shifted from the interface to the code. Instead of positioning and keyframing an object by hand, I wrote scripts that produced the same result programmatically. The output looked identical, but the process was completely different, and that contrast turned out to be the real lesson of the course. Once the behavior lived in a script, it could be repeated, tweaked, and applied to many objects at once without redoing the work manually each time.
